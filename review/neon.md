## 1. Pointer Accuracy & Stability

### Root-Cause Audit of Current Implementation (`frontend/phone/pointer.js`)

1. **Aspect-ratio distortion (`1 / cos(pitch)` stretch) in `toCanvas()` (`pointer.js:111-116`):**  
   `aimDirection()` correctly computes the back-of-phone unit vector $\mathbf{v} = R_z(\alpha) R_x(\beta) R_y(\gamma)(0,0,-1)^T$, and `yawPitch()` converts it to spherical `(yaw, pitch)`. However, `toCanvas()` divides raw `wrap180(yaw - yaw0)` directly by `fov`. When a player sits in a lecture theatre pointing **up** at a projector (`pitch ≈ 25°–45°`), azimuth degrees shrink physically by $\cos(\text{pitch})$, stretching horizontal strokes by **10%–41%** and turning circles into wide ovals. Because `scoreLocal.js:46` normalises isotropically (one scalar RMS radius `r`), any horizontal/vertical aspect stretch directly penalises accuracy.
2. **Gimbal lock at $\beta \approx 90^\circ$ + broken fallback (`pointer.js:130-198`):**  
   Players are instructed to *"Hold your phone up like a camera"* (`index.html:121`), which puts the phone directly on the W3C Z-X'-Y'' Euler singularity ($\beta = 90^\circ$). Even though `aimDirection()` cancels the singularity *in exact math*, mobile OSes decompose quaternions into `(alpha, beta, gamma)` first — crossing $\beta = 89.5^\circ \leftrightarrow 90.5^\circ$ often causes `alpha` and `gamma` to flip $180^\circ$ one frame apart, creating a sharp spike.  
   Worse, the current fallback has **three concrete bugs**:
   - **Snap-back (`pointer.js:131-151`):** `useRateFallbackUntil` integrates `rotationRate` into `rawX/rawY` for 350 ms without updating `yaw0/pitch0`, then `handleDeviceOrientation` abruptly overwrites `rawX/rawY` at 351 ms, causing a visible jump.
   - **One-shot lock bug (`pointer.js:180-181`):** If `devicemotion` fires before `deviceorientation` (common on iOS), `!hasOrientationEvent` is true on tick 2, sets `hasOrientationEvent = true`, and then **permanently disables** the fallback on tick 3 even if `deviceorientation` never arrives!
   - **Unverified iOS vs Android `rotationRate` sign (`pointer.js:182-183`):** iOS Safari and Android Chrome have historically disagreed on `rotationRate` axis signs on several OS builds, so blindly integrating `-r.gamma` / `-r.beta` can move the cursor backwards during fallback.
3. **Frame-rate-dependent smoothing (`SMOOTHING = 0.35`, `pointer.js:162-163`):**  
   Fixed per-event EMA `sx += 0.35 * (nx - sx)` behaves very differently across devices: at 60 Hz (iPhone) $\tau \approx 39\text{ ms}$ (lets 6–10 Hz hand tremor through); at 20–30 Hz (throttled Android) $\tau \approx 100\text{ ms}$ (rounds off star tips and triangle corners).
4. **Calibration during thumb-tap jolt (`phone.js:462-480`, `pointer.js:240-264`):**  
   Calibrating over the final 500 ms before GO captures the exact moment players lift their arm to the starting vertex or tap their thumb onto the glass (5°–10° jolt).
5. **FOV (`40°`) & ergonomics (`backend/constants.js:5`, `pointer.js:17`):**  
   Because `scoreLocal.js` is scale-invariant, `FOV` only affects (a) the `r >= 0.02` validity floor (`0.8°` at 40°), (b) lobby test-dot feel, and (c) the `|x| > 5` glitch filter. Sitting elbows-in in a lecture theatre, a 40° sweep feels slightly sluggish in the lobby test box; `32°–35°` encourages comfortable wrist arcs without pushing the phone into extreme angles.
6. **iOS vs Android platform traps:**
   - **Critical iOS refresh bug (`phone.js:807-818`):** On reload, `socket.on('connect')` silently rejoins via `sessionStorage` and enters the lobby **without** calling `SkyPointer.askMotion()` (which requires a user tap). iOS resets `DeviceOrientationEvent.requestPermission()` on reload, leaving refreshed iPhones permanently stuck with dead motion and no button to re-grant it.
   - **Android compass interference:** Some Android devices emit `e.absolute === true` on `deviceorientation` (magnetometer-fused). Near lecture-hall steel/projectors, compass corrections cause sudden 20°–50° yaw warps.
   - **Sample-rate & `setInterval` jitter (`pointer.js:334`):** `setInterval(33)` pushes duplicate points when stationary and misses fast corner peaks between ticks.

| Approach | Accuracy & Stability (10–15 s round) | Main Risk | Verdict |
